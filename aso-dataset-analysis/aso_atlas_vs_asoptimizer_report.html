ASO Atlas vs ASOptimizer

Comprehensive Dataset Analysis for Gapmer ASO Design

TL;DR

ASOptimizer and ASO Atlas are comparable datasets. Both contain knockdown efficacy measurements, ASO sequences, and chemical modification information. The datasets share 13,779 identical sequences and 11 genes, with a Pearson correlation of 0.54 for efficacy values on overlapping sequences.

Key difference: ASO Atlas lacks SMILES molecular structures, which ASOptimizer uses for its graph neural network-based chemical engineering model. However, SMILES can be computationally generated from ASO Atlas's position-level modification data (sequence + modification positions → SMILES) using cheminformatics libraries like RDKit.

Bottom line: ASO Atlas could be used to expand ASOptimizer's training data after SMILES generation, or directly for sequence-only models.

Key Insights for Gapmer ASO Design

1. Sequence Motifs for High Efficacy

  • High-efficacy ASOs show slight enrichment for G/C content at wing positions
  • Gap region (central DNA portion) tolerates more variation
  • Avoid long poly-G stretches (G-quartets) and repetitive sequences
  • 5' end nucleotide composition may influence RNase H cleavage efficiency

2. Chemistry Selection Guidelines

  • MOE gapmers (5-10-5): Best balance of efficacy, tolerability, and manufacturability
  • cEt gapmers (3-10-3): Higher potency, useful for challenging targets
  • Mixed MOE/cEt: Consider for optimizing binding affinity without toxicity
  • LNA: Reserve for targets requiring highest affinity; monitor for hepatotoxicity
  • Phosphorothioate (PS): Essential for in vivo stability; present in >95% of samples

3. Target Region Recommendations

  • 3' UTR: Generally more accessible, common target region
  • CDS: Can work well, especially near start codon
  • Avoid: Highly structured regions, SNP sites, splice junctions (unless targeting splicing)
  • Use RNA structure prediction to identify accessible regions

4. Dataset Complementarity

  • ASO Atlas: Larger, more diverse gene coverage (343 genes), better for general training
  • ASOptimizer: Includes SMILES representations and detailed chemical patterns, better for chemistry optimization
  • Overlap: 13,779 shared sequences enable cross-validation
  • Combined: Use both datasets for robust model training
